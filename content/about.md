I'm an infrastructure engineer with nearly 20 years of experience keeping systems running. Data centres, virtual machines, networks, and cloud platforms. Whatever the workload runs on, the job is the same: make it reliable, make it secure, and make it something the next person can understand.

Most of my work these days lands on AWS, but I started long before the cloud was the default answer. Racking hardware, wiring networks, and running things on metal teaches you where the abstractions leak. That perspective still shapes how I design cloud systems now.

## What I do

I help organisations design, automate, and run their infrastructure. That covers cloud architecture, Infrastructure as Code, networking, and the operational discipline that keeps a platform healthy once it's live. I've worked across AWS, on-premise environments, and Kubernetes, often bridging the two.

## Why I write

A lot of technical writing is either too shallow to use or so deep it forgets the point. I write for the people who have to build and run the thing, not pass an exam about it. Real problems, honest trade-offs, and what I learned from getting it wrong the first time.
